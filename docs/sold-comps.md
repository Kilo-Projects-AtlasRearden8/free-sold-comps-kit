# Sold-Comps Worksheet

Resale price research made simple. Record recently **sold** comparable listings (not active listings — those are asking prices, not proof of value) and let the worksheet compute the average, median, low, high, and a suggested list price.

Printable. No account needed. Works offline. © 2026 Asdale Dynamics, LLC.

## How to use

1. Open `sold-comps.html` in any browser.
2. Add one row per recently sold comparable listing. Match condition as closely as possible (see the condition-grading guide).
3. Paste sold listings from eBay, Amazon, Poshmark, Mercari, or local sales. Prices are what buyers actually paid.
4. Totals compute automatically as you type.
5. Use the suggested price (median of sold comps) as a starting point, then adjust up/down for your item's condition, included accessories, and shipping costs.
6. Print this page (Ctrl+P / Cmd+P) before you list or ship.

## Fields

| Field | What to enter |
|-------|---------------|
| # | Auto-numbered |
| Item / title | Title of the sold comparable item |
| Condition | Condition tier (Like New, Very Good, Good, Acceptable, Poor) |
| Sold price | What the buyer actually paid |
| Date sold | Date the sale completed |
| Marketplace | eBay, Amazon, Poshmark, Mercari, local, etc. |
| Notes | Accessories included, shipping terms, flaws, etc. |

## What the worksheet computes

- **Comps** — number of valid sold listings entered
- **Average** — mean of sold prices
- **Median** — middle sold price (the suggested list price)
- **Low / High** — minimum and maximum sold price
- **Suggested list price** — median of sold comps

## Pricing rule of thumb

Start at the median sold price, then adjust for condition and demand:

- Like New: near top of the used range
- Very Good: slightly below Like New
- Good: mid-range used price (most common)
- Acceptable: discounted to move
- Poor / for parts: lowest tier

Always verify current marketplace fee schedules before pricing (see the fee estimator).
